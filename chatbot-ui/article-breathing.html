5 Simple Breathing Exercises for Stress Relief

Quick and effective breathing techniques you can use anywhere to reduce stress and promote calm.

The Power of Breath

Your breath is one of the most powerful tools you have for managing stress and anxiety. Unlike your heartbeat or digestion, breathing is both automatic and controllable—making it a perfect bridge between your conscious and unconscious mind.

When you're stressed, your breathing becomes shallow and rapid. By consciously slowing and deepening your breath, you activate your parasympathetic nervous system, triggering your body's natural relaxation response.

1. Box Breathing (4-4-4-4)

Best for: Anxiety, focus, and quick stress relief

Also known as square breathing, this technique is used by Navy SEALs to stay calm under pressure.

How to do it:

  • Sit comfortably with your feet flat on the floor
  • Exhale completely through your mouth
  • Inhale through your nose for 4 counts
  • Hold your breath for 4 counts
  • Exhale through your mouth for 4 counts
  • Hold empty for 4 counts
  • Repeat for 4-5 cycles

Pro tip: Visualize tracing a square as you breathe—up on the inhale, across on the hold, down on the exhale, and across on the empty hold.

2. 4-7-8 Breathing

Best for: Falling asleep, calming anxiety, and reducing anger

Developed by Dr. Andrew Weil, this technique is sometimes called "the natural tranquilizer for the nervous system."

How to do it:

  • Place the tip of your tongue against the ridge behind your upper front teeth
  • Exhale completely through your mouth, making a whoosh sound
  • Close your mouth and inhale quietly through your nose for 4 counts
  • Hold your breath for 7 counts
  • Exhale completely through your mouth for 8 counts, making a whoosh sound
  • Repeat for 4 cycles

Important: The ratio (4:7:8) matters more than the exact speed. If 4 seconds feels too long, try 2-3.5-4 instead.

3. Diaphragmatic Breathing (Belly Breathing)

Best for: Deep relaxation, reducing blood pressure, and improving oxygen flow

This fundamental technique engages your diaphragm, allowing for fuller, more efficient breaths.

How to do it:

  • Lie on your back or sit comfortably
  • Place one hand on your chest and one on your belly
  • Breathe in slowly through your nose, feeling your belly rise while your chest stays relatively still
  • Exhale slowly through pursed lips, feeling your belly fall
  • Continue for 5-10 minutes

Why it works: Most people breathe shallowly from their chest. Belly breathing uses your full lung capacity, delivering more oxygen and triggering relaxation.

4. Alternate Nostril Breathing (Nadi Shodhana)

Best for: Balancing energy, improving focus, and calming the mind

This yogic breathing technique balances the left and right hemispheres of your brain.

How to do it:

  • Sit comfortably with your spine straight
  • Use your right thumb to close your right nostril
  • Inhale slowly through your left nostril
  • Close your left nostril with your right ring finger
  • Release your thumb and exhale through your right nostril
  • Inhale through your right nostril
  • Close your right nostril and exhale through your left
  • This completes one cycle. Repeat for 5-10 cycles

Note: This might feel awkward at first, but it becomes natural with practice.

5. Resonant Breathing (Coherent Breathing)

Best for: Heart rate variability, emotional balance, and sustained calm

This technique involves breathing at a rate of 5 breaths per minute, which research shows maximizes heart rate variability.

How to do it:

  • Sit or lie down comfortably
  • Inhale through your nose for 6 counts
  • Exhale through your nose or mouth for 6 counts
  • Continue this pattern for 10-20 minutes

Variation: Some people prefer 5-count breaths (5 in, 5 out). Experiment to find what feels most natural.

Tips for Success

Start Small

Even 2-3 minutes of breathing exercises can make a difference. Don't feel pressured to do long sessions right away.

Practice Regularly

Like any skill, breathing techniques become more effective with practice. Try to incorporate them into your daily routine.

Use Them Preventatively

Don't wait until you're overwhelmed. Practice when you're calm so the techniques are familiar when stress hits.

Find Your Favorite

Different techniques work for different people and situations. Experiment to find what resonates with you.

Combine with Other Practices

Breathing exercises pair beautifully with meditation, yoga, or progressive muscle relaxation.

When to Use Each Technique

  • Feeling anxious? Try Box Breathing or 4-7-8
  • Can't sleep? Use 4-7-8 Breathing
  • Need to focus? Try Box Breathing or Alternate Nostril
  • Feeling overwhelmed? Use Diaphragmatic Breathing
  • Want overall balance? Practice Resonant Breathing

The Science Behind It

Controlled breathing works because it directly influences your autonomic nervous system. When you slow your breath:

  • Your heart rate decreases
  • Blood pressure lowers
  • Stress hormones like cortisol reduce
  • Your vagus nerve is stimulated, promoting calm
  • Oxygen delivery to your brain improves

Your Breath, Your Anchor

The beauty of breathing exercises is that your breath is always with you. No special equipment, no apps, no perfect conditions required—just you and your breath.

Start with one technique today. Practice it for just a few minutes. Notice how you feel. Over time, these simple exercises can become powerful tools for navigating life's challenges with greater calm and clarity.
